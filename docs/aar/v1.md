# After-Action Review — Operation SCOUT v1

- **Campaign:** Operation SCOUT, Phases 0–5
- **Opened:** 2026-04-21 (Phase 0 mobilization)
- **v1 released:** 2026-07-06, `v0.1.0`
- **AAR filed:** 2026-08-14 by chief-of-staff
- **Phase 5 status:** closed with this document

---

## 1. What was asked, and what was delivered

> **Commander's intent:** Make finding and acting on my projects fast,
> composable, and portable across my machines.

Delivered: a Rust binary that indexes a tree into WAL SQLite, ranks it by
fuzzy-match quality blended with frecency, presents a TUI picker, and
executes declarative TOML actions behind a first-run trust prompt — plus
an installer, a guarded shell wrapper, CI tripwires, release machinery,
a diagnostics subcommand, and five signed ADRs that bind the code.

The intent's three words each got an answer. *Fast*: the 100k-path
budget (under 30 s wall, under 100 MB RSS) has held since Phase 2 and is
now enforced in CI. *Composable*: the print seam and `scout query` let
scout be used from a shell without the TUI. *Portable*: `install.sh`,
the shell snippet, TOML config designed for dotfiles, and musl release
tarballs for two architectures.

Six phases ran to completion. Nothing in the campaign plan was
abandoned; two things were struck later by commander decision (remote
filesystems, the preview pane), and both were struck deliberately rather
than dropped.

## 2. What worked

**The ADR-before-code rule.** Four ADRs signed before Phase 2 meant the
line officers never re-litigated ranking, dependencies, threat model, or
config schema mid-flight. When Phase 3 ran three sectors in parallel,
they agreed with each other because they were all reading the same
documents. The cost was one phase of pure deliberation; the return was
every phase after it.

**Tripwires, when they actually ran.** See §3 for the qualifier, but the
gates found real defects that human review missed — repeatedly, and in
the work of the people who wrote the gates.

**Sector ownership.** Merge conflicts across five parallel officers were
essentially nil, because file ownership was declared before anyone
wrote code rather than negotiated after.

**Deviations were recorded, not hidden.** Five deviations from the OPORD
were filed in HANDOFF as they happened (sync search rather than a worker
pipeline; no diff view on trust change; `process_group` rather than
`setsid`; `unix_now` visibility widened cross-sector; an extra `scout
query` subcommand). All five are ratified by this AAR: each was the
right call, and the record of *why* survived long enough for this
document to check them.

## 3. What went wrong — seven lessons

These are ordered by how much they should change future behaviour.

### 3.1 A gate that does not run is not a gate

`smoke_100k_paths_under_budget` — the test that guards the Phase 2
performance checkpoint, the one number in the campaign plan with a hard
threshold — was marked `#[ignore]` and CI ran plain `cargo test`. It had
**never executed in CI**. Likewise the repository was never
`rustfmt`-clean and had no `fmt` step, and `cargo audit` ran only on
push, so an advisory published the day after the last push sat
undetected for five weeks.

Three separate instances of the same shape: the gate existed, was
correct, and was wired to nothing. Writing the check is the easy half.

**Carry forward:** every gate must be observed failing at least once. A
gate never seen red is indistinguishable from a gate not wired up.

### 3.2 The gates found what the reviewers could not

`cargo deny`, on its very first real run, discovered that
`nucleo-matcher` — a *signed roster crate*, slot 6 — is MPL-2.0 and was
absent from ADR-002's own licence allow-list. Four council officers and
the commander reviewed and signed that ADR. The tool found the hole in
the doctrine that authored it.

Separately, shadow-review found a real filename-injection vulnerability
at the print seam (`{name}`, `{query}`, `{env}` unquoted on a
wrapper-`eval`'d line) that survived the council, the sector that wrote
it, and the commander's daily use.

**Carry forward:** mechanical checks are not a lesser form of review.
They are a *different* form, and on questions of fact — what licence is
this, what version resolves, is this string quoted — they are simply
better than people, including careful people.

### 3.3 A deferral is a judgement about a cost, and costs move

`unicode-width` was deferred to v2 on the stated grounds that it was off
the ADR-002 roster. That reason was quoted forward for five weeks. One
`cargo tree -i` showed the crate was **already in the shipped graph
twice**, under `ratatui` directly and via `unicode-truncate`. Admitting
it moved the transitive ceiling by zero.

The same note also misjudged severity, calling the defect a cosmetic
misalignment. Measured, character counting produced path columns of 43,
43, 49 and 51 over a mixed CJK/ASCII tree, and the renderer clipped the
widest row's basename mid-name — destroying the most useful part of the
row, which left-anchored truncation exists specifically to protect.

**Carry forward:** re-price a deferral before quoting it, and re-derive
its severity too. A severity label written by someone about to defer the
work was written by someone motivated to keep it small. File deferrals
with a re-check trigger so they expire instead of calcifying.

### 3.4 Writing a prohibition into doctrine does not stop you violating it

ADR-006 §Alternatives 4 rejects a `doctor` that repairs what it finds,
in as many words: *"a repair tool that runs in the same breath as a
diagnosis destroys the evidence"*. The first implementation of `doctor`,
written immediately after that sentence by the same author, called
`index::pragma::open` — which creates directories, runs migrations, and
rename-and-rebuilds a corrupt database. `doctor` was repairing the fault
while reporting it.

The corrupt-index drill caught it: the run reported a healthy empty
database, because the open had just fabricated one. No amount of
re-reading the ADR would have found this, because the author had
*already read it* — they wrote it.

**Carry forward:** doctrine constrains the decision, not the hands. Only
a drill against a real failing state tests the implementation. Prefer a
test that observes the system from outside the process (`tests/doctor.rs`
now asserts that a fresh sandbox comes back with nothing created and a
corrupt database is byte-identical afterwards).

### 3.5 A test can assert the wrong quantity and pass forever

`truncation_keeps_tail` asserted `cells.len()` after truncation. Cell
count and column count are equal for ASCII and diverge for everything
else, so the assertion passed with the width bug fully present. The test
was not weak; it was measuring a different thing than the one that
mattered.

**Carry forward:** when a bug survives a suite, ask what the test
measured rather than assuming coverage was missing. The new tests assert
columns, and say in a comment why the obvious assertion is wrong.

### 3.6 The documented path was never the drilled path

`install.sh` was committed mode `100644`. `./install.sh` — the first
command in the README — returned `Permission denied` on any fresh clone.
The Phase 4 "fresh-machine checkpoint" reported success because it
invoked `sh install.sh`, which is not what the documentation tells
anyone to type.

**Carry forward:** a fresh-machine drill must execute the documented
commands verbatim, copy-pasted, not a working equivalent. The gap
between "the thing works" and "the instructions work" is exactly where
first impressions are lost.

### 3.7 Scaffolding acquires no users on its own

`ops/logs/` was created in Phase 0, preserved by two `.gitignore` rules,
and never written to by anything, ever. Removed in the 2026-08-14 scrub.

**Carry forward:** scaffold when the consumer exists, not in
anticipation of one.

## 5. Promotions

Per `ops/CAMPAIGN.md` Phase 5, promotions expand a sector for v2.

**Pioneers — promoted.** Sector expands to own *every* gate: the fmt
check, the perf gate, the supply-chain schedule, and the release
workflow, plus the standing obligation in §3.1 that each gate be
observed failing once. Pioneers' CI tripwires are the single highest-
return artefact of the campaign; §3.2 is their citation.

**3rd Rifles (TUI) — promoted.** Takes ADR-007 (Spotlight
presentation), the flagship of v2, and with it the disambiguation logic
that is the substance of that ADR. Their Phase 3 visual pass and the
`render.rs` pure-function discipline are why ADR-005 and ADR-007 are
testable at all.

**Council-security — reinstated as a standing officer, not a
phase-bound one.** This is the structural correction the AAR most
insists on. Security's absence is felt in three separate places:
ADR-005's bidi question, ADR-006's unreviewed allowlist, and the print
seam injection that shadow-review caught after the council had
disbanded. A council that dissolves at phase close cannot review the
work that follows the phase. Security reviews every ADR and every
change touching the print seam, trust store, or render boundary,
regardless of phase.

**Engineers — commended, not promoted.** The trust model and the closed
placeholder set held under adversarial review; the injection was in
their sector and was theirs to have caught. Both facts are true and
neither cancels the other.

**Council-quartermaster — commended.** ADR-002 is the most-cited
document in the campaign. The MPL-2.0 gap (§3.2) is a finding about
review method, not about the officer.

## 6. v2 opening objectives

In dependency order. Every item needs its ADR before code, per
CLAUDE.md §4.

1. **ADR-007 Spotlight presentation** — drafted, awaiting signature.
   3rd Rifles. The flagship: name-first rows, minimal disambiguating
   context, a compact surface, no ranking metadata in the row.
2. **Dependency refresh** — Quartermaster + Pioneers. `ratatui`
   0.26 → 0.30 and `crossterm` 0.27 → 0.29 move together and gate the UI
   work; `rusqlite` 0.31 → 0.40; `thiserror` 2.x and `toml` 1.x are
   separate decisions ADR-002 already flagged. The `unicode-width` `0.1`
   pin is released by the ratatui bump and not before (ADR-005).
3. **ADR-003 revision — display integrity** — council-security. §4
   above.
4. **Machine-readable output** — one ADR covering `scout query` and
   `scout doctor` together. Both have the same gap and it should be
   decided once. `serde_json` is off the roster until a JSON mode ships;
   a delimited format needs no dependency at all.
5. **Keybinding dispatch beyond `enter`** — ADR-004 §6 already reserves
   `tab`, `alt-e`, `alt-c`, `ctrl-o` and the chord forms, and the loader
   warns that they are recognised but not dispatched. The runway is laid.

Explicitly **not** in v2: remote and mounted filesystems (struck by the
commander, 2026-08-14 — do not draft an ADR); embedded interpreters
(ADR-002: "not for v1, not for v2 without a redesign"); parallelism,
conditionals or loops inside the action schema (ADR-004: it would turn
the action loader into a runtime).

## 7. Closing

Six phases, five signed ADRs plus two more since, 69 tests, zero
outstanding advisories, one released binary that the commander drives
daily.

The campaign's most instructive finding is not any single bug. It is
that on every occasion where a mechanical check and a human review
disagreed about a matter of fact, the mechanical check was right — and
that the checks kept finding holes in the very doctrine that had
mandated them. v2's structural bet, in §5, is to stop treating review as
an event that ends at a phase boundary.

Phase 5 closed. Operation SCOUT v1 complete.
